# English discussion deck — speaker notes

Slides retain result tables and a graph alongside one discussion point per page. Numerical interpretation is delivered orally. References are collected on slide 15.

## 01. Anticipating numeric conflicts

Introduce the research problem: anticipating resource conflicts while keeping planning fast. The method remains open.

## 02. Robots need useful plans. Quickly.

Motivate this with a robot that must plan under battery, fuel or capacity constraints. A useful plan must arrive in time to act.

## 03. Fast guidance can miss resource interactions.

Our planner comparison motivates this trade-off. It does not establish that every more expensive heuristic is more informative or produces better plans.

## 04. A domain name is not a difficulty model.

Discuss how goals, initial resources and topology activate different interactions within the same domain. Avoid treating the domain name as a difficulty label.

## 05. Tighter constraints can help search.

Logistics provides an example consistent with earlier pruning. This is not a universal rule: tighter constraints also made other configurations harder.

## 06. A feasible action can lead to an infeasible future.

Barman motivates the difference between immediate blockage and a cumulative shortage. The stock comparison changes deficit size too, so it does not isolate detection depth.

## 07. Misleading choices can survive too long.

The synthetic pilot motivates this hypothesis. Its false-finite labels use an external relaxation, not an instrumented Metric-FF heuristic. The micro experiments are small unsolvable chains; extra reachable depth alone can increase exhaustive search.

## 08. An extra branch matters only if search follows it.

Extra side branches did not increase expanded states in every configuration. Those branches are structural dead ends, so the experiment does not establish persistent numeric ambiguity or identify the pruning mechanism.

## 09. Bring future resource conflicts into current search guidance.

Define the target phenomenon as locally applicable choices with no feasible continuation, which the selected heuristic fails to recognize early. Solvable tasks with both good and bad choices are the next important test.

## 10. Richer numeric reasoning already exists.

Explain the role of relaxation, subgoaling, LP constraints and abstraction. Their supported numeric fragments and computational costs differ; they are candidates, not interchangeable modules.

## 11. Selective evaluation already has a foundation.

State-level selection is established work. The proposed research angle is a low-cost signal of misleading numeric relaxation, evaluated against generic selectors and simple baselines.

## 12. Use a cheap signal to allocate additional reasoning.

Present this as one possible method, not a committed algorithm. Potential-based features, limited lookahead and semantic priors remain open options. A risk prediction alone is not a sound pruning certificate.

## 13. Where does additional reasoning actually help?

First establish whether an additional heuristic actually helps on the same states. Compare solvability or ranking decisions and evaluation costs in a shared search implementation.

## 14. Can we predict misleading guidance before the conflict?

Invite discussion about predictive signals and when their cost is justified. End with the research question, not a claim of a validated solution.
